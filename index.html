1.0Executive Summary

Of the 22 candidates entered for the 2026 NECO BECE under School No. 0201437, 20 sat the examination and 2 were absent. The cohort achieved a credit-and-above rate of 77.5% against an average grade point of 3.11 out of 5.0.

This report presents the Board with a summary of cohort performance, explains the circumstances of the two absent candidates for the record, identifies areas of academic concern, and sets out the strategic intervention measures management has already put in motion — including a mandatory Holiday Coaching programme for the three candidates whose results require the closest support ahead of their transition into Senior Secondary 1 (SS1).

4.0Matter for the Board: Candidate Absenteeism

Two candidates — both female, both validly registered — did not present themselves for any paper. Management sets out the circumstances of each below for the Board's record.

4.1  Ismail Zainab Oyinkansola

Reg. No. 2630056961BD  ·  DOB 23/04/2012  ·  Female

Zainab has continued her education with the school remotely since her family's migration to Saudi Arabia. Her parents had planned for her to travel back to Nigeria ahead of the examination period; however, delays in the renewal of the family's visa and the associated travel arrangements made it impossible for her to arrive in time to sit any paper. The school remained in contact with the family throughout this period and was kept informed of the travel difficulties as they arose.

4.2  Yahaya Rukayyah Mukhtar

Reg. No. 2630056998JG  ·  DOB 11/11/2011  ·  Female

Rukayyah's family relocated from Lagos during the course of the term. The school maintained ongoing communication with her parent, who repeatedly assured the school of the family's intention to bring her back in time for the examination. Regrettably, this return did not materialise before the examination commenced, and Rukayyah was consequently unable to sit any paper.

Board Note

Both candidates remain on the school's register. Management will continue to engage both families regarding academic continuity and will pursue any resit or make-up arrangements available through the examination body. The Board is invited to note this matter — see Section 7.0(ii).

5.0Areas of Concern

Two distinct concerns emerged from the results and are being addressed through the intervention plan in Section 6.0:

L2
Weak indigenous-language performance. Yoruba returned the cohort's lowest average (2.31/5) with 3 outright fails, and Arabic was the second-weakest subject (2.40/5). This is the school's most pressing subject-level concern going into SS1.
!
Three candidates with low overall averages. Ahmed Fawaz Lateef (2.33/5), Yusuf El-Amin Alogi (2.50/5), and Mustapha Abdulrazaq Firdaus (2.17/5, including two outright fails in Basic Science & Technology and Pre-Vocational Studies) recorded the cohort's three lowest averages and require close, structured support before joining SS1.

6.0Strategic Intervention Plan

Management wishes to assure the Board that strategic interventions have already been planned to support the candidates identified with weak performance, ahead of their transition into SS1.

6.1 · Priority Candidates

Mandatory Holiday Coaching (Summer Coaching)

The three candidates with the cohort's lowest averages —

Ahmed Fawaz LateefYusuf El-Amin AlogiMustapha Abdulrazaq Firdaus

— have been identified as priority cases and are mandated to attend the school's Strategic Intervention Classes during the Holiday Coaching (Summer Coaching) period. Individual advisory notices explaining the requirement have already been issued to their parents/guardians, and their attendance and progress will be tracked directly by the Head of School's office to ensure they are properly prepared for the increased academic demands of SS1.

6.2 · Subject-Level Remediation

Targeted Yoruba & Arabic Support

Given the cohort-wide weakness in Yoruba and Arabic, management will extend focused revision sessions in both subjects to all candidates progressing into SS1, in addition to the individual coaching described above, to close this gap before the language demands of the senior school curriculum increase.

6.3 · Oversight

Monitoring & Reporting Back to the Board

The Head of School's office will monitor attendance and progress throughout the Holiday Coaching period and report outcomes to the Board at the start of the next academic session, including any adjustment to the intervention list arising from subsequent internal assessment.

Management respectfully requests that the Board:
